Key EDA Finding

Grouping the 132 merged trading days by same-day market direction shows a clear split: days the market closed up (target = 1) had an average rule-based sentiment score of ~9.2, versus ~2.8 on days it closed down (target = 0). News tone and same-day price direction move together, but this reflects shared same-day causes (e.g. an earnings report driving both the headline and the price) rather than sentiment leading price.

Part A ,  Same-Day Accuracy vs. Baseline

The hand-written rule (predict UP if sentiment_score > 0) scored 62.88% accuracy against a 50.76% majority-class baseline ,  a real but modest same-day co-movement signal. Because both sentiment_score and target are computed from the same trading day, this is not a forecast: it says today's news tone lines up with today's price direction, not that it predicts tomorrow's.

Part B ,  Best Next-Day Model vs. Baseline

After fixing the leakage (shifting the target to next_day_target and using a chronological 100/25-day train/test split), the majority-class baseline on the test period was 56.0%. Of four models compared ,  Logistic Regression (48%), untuned Random Forest (40%), XGBoost (36%), and a GridSearchCV-tuned Random Forest (44%, best CV accuracy 53.75%) ,  Logistic Regression was the best next-day performer at 48%, still below the 56% baseline. Feature importances from the tuned Random Forest also ranked price-history features (high_low_spread, movement_lag1) above every sentiment feature, reinforcing that sentiment does not out-predict simple price history at a next-day horizon.

What I Would Do Differently With More Time

With more time I would extend the news window well past six months ,  125 usable rows and a 25-day test set make every reported accuracy noisy, so a 40% vs. 48% gap between models is within sampling variance, not a real difference. I would also pull sector-specific headlines to see if a narrower sentiment signal (e.g. banking news vs. Bank NIFTY) predicts better than broad-market sentiment, and try a proper NLP embedding (VADER/FinBERT) instead of a hand-built word list, since the word-list-size experiment already showed the scoring approach is sensitive to vocabulary choice (140/145 words performed worse than 59/53). Finally, I'd add walk-forward (rolling-origin) validation instead of a single 80/20 split, since one fixed test window can't tell me whether 48% is a real edge or luck.
